# 06. The objections that land

*The sceptic's best case, made properly. If you are going to defend this argument, defend it against these, because the cheap dismissals in the previous file are not what will actually beat you.*

Writing this file well is the price of the previous one. A repo that catalogues bad objections and then hides the good ones is doing advocacy, not thinking.

---

## 1. The cadence may be an artefact of the selection

**The objection.** The eight-rung ladder is offered as evidence for a 6,000-year beat. But the rungs were chosen by people who already held the beat, from a palaeo record with dating uncertainty in the hundreds to thousands of years, and where a rung was empty a candidate was found for it. Under those conditions a regular spacing is close to guaranteed regardless of whether a driver exists.

**Why it lands, and where it does not.** Two of eight rungs are in the accepted inventory. Three more (Gothenburg ~12 ka, Hilina Pali ~19 ka, Lake Mungo ~26 ka) are real published proposals that have not been confirmed, so the harsh version of this objection overreaches: most of the ladder is not invented. Two rungs do have nothing behind them at any status, the 48 ka slot and "Michoacan". But the objection survives in a sharper form: the complete set of excursions proposed for the last 30 kyr lands at roughly 2.7, 10, 12, 13, 19, 26 and 26 ka. That is a scatter, with two candidates nearly on top of each other and a long gap where the ladder wants a rung. You cannot accept the unconfirmed events that fill the ladder and reject the unconfirmed events that break it.

**How much damage.** Substantial, and to the argument's best feature. The metronome is the persuasive part. If the metronome is in the list rather than in the rock, the persuasion was circular.

**The strongest available reply.** The excursion record genuinely is sparse and biased towards whatever happens to preserve and be sampled, so absence of a signal is weak evidence. That reply defends against falsification but does not restore the cadence as *evidence*. Both things are true, and the honest position is to stop offering the ladder as proof while continuing to think the question is open.

## 2. The mechanism has no model

**The objection.** Every other link in the chain is a plausibility argument. There is no quantitative model of the galactic current sheet delivering enough mass to a main-sequence star's atmosphere to build an ejectable shell, no calculated accumulation rate, no threshold, no energy budget, no predicted spectrum. The books concede the solar-wind stunting mechanism cannot currently be modelled.

**Why it lands.** Without numbers the claim cannot be wrong in any specific way, which means it cannot be tested. A model that produced a required dust column density would immediately be checkable against interstellar medium measurements, and it would either survive or not. Its absence is not a small gap; it is the difference between a hypothesis and a story.

**The strongest available reply.** New mechanisms often begin as plausibility arguments and get modelled later, and some of the enabling results are recent (companion-free Type Ia, environment-triggered outburst). That is fair as an argument for patience. It is not an argument for present belief, and treating it as one is where the frontier most often overreaches.

## 3. The specific numbers do not survive checking

**The objection.** Claims that carry weight in the books are wrong against the measured record. "5% of the field lost per decade" against a measured ~5% per century. "Down 20-25%" against a measured ~9% since 1840. Continued pole acceleration against a measured deceleration to ~35 km/yr confirmed in the 2025 World Magnetic Model.

**Why it lands.** Not because a wrong number falsifies a hypothesis, but because these particular numbers are the observational core of the "it is happening now" case. If the field is declining at 5% per century and the pole is slowing, the urgency argument loses its instrument reading.

**The strongest available reply.** The global dipole average is not the whole story: the South Atlantic Anomaly weakens far faster locally, particle penetration into the radiation belts has deepened, and local rates are what matter for exposure. That is a genuinely good reply. It also means the global figures should never have been quoted the way they were. Full ledger in [`08-numbers.md`](08-numbers.md).

## 4. The forecast window has moved

**The objection.** *The Next End of the World* (2020) put grid-threatening field loss around 2030-2040 and the reversal moment at 2040-2060, while allowing that acceleration could compress it to months or a few years. The 2022 Supplement has the event "this century". Dates that recede are the classic signature of an unfalsifiable forecast.

**Why it lands.** It is the strongest available case for the pseudoscience charge, and unlike the ad hominem version it is about method. A forecast that survives every observation by widening has stopped doing work.

**The strongest available reply.** The books do state the timing as speculative in their own words, and give rival cycle lengths from other researchers rather than a single confident figure. Hedged forecasts are legitimate. What is not legitimate is presenting the hedged version in the text and the confident version on the card.

## 5. The wrapper is not costless

**The objection.** The CIA cover-up chapter, the Apollo mission logo read as the four horsemen, Nibiru identified with Scholz's star, Atlantis, the Texas rock wall, cannibalism in the cities. These are not incidental. They are in the same book, arguing from the same evidentiary standard, and if that standard produces the horsemen reading it is not a standard that can then certify the isotope argument.

**Why it lands.** This is the objection the frontier most needs to answer and least often does. It is not guilt by association; it is a question about method consistency. If the same author applies one level of rigour to *Nature* citations and another to a mission patch, a reader has no way to tell which mode a given page is in.

**The strongest available reply.** Books can mix registers, and the physical claims carry their own citations that can be checked independently of the mythological ones. True, and it puts the whole burden on the reader to do the separation. Which is precisely what a repo like this one is for, and is the honest reason it needs to exist.

## 6. Coincidence-counting cuts both ways

**The objection.** "Too many coincidences to ignore" is a real form of inference, and it is also the standard engine of conspiracy reasoning. Without a prior probability and a count of the coincidences that were looked for and not found, the argument has no denominator. Nobody lists the planets that did not change or the nearby stars that did not flare.

**Why it lands.** It is a structural criticism, not a nitpick, and it applies to the argument's own preferred move. Barnard's Star, Proxima and AD Leonis are offered as a sequence towards the Sun. The search space of nearby stars, over decades of improving instruments that would find new variability anywhere they were pointed, is not given.

**The strongest available reply.** The prediction was made in advance in at least one case: a Harvard-Smithsonian critic asked whether nearby stars in the direction of the galactic centre should be reacting, and examples were then found. Advance prediction is worth more than post-hoc fitting. It is worth much more if the negative cases are reported too.

---

## What survives all six

Not the mechanism. Not the cadence. Not the timing.

What survives is narrower and more durable: **the Sun's demonstrated ceiling is far above the instrumental record, the magnetic shield is measurably weakening, and the consequences of the pair are underpriced in public planning.** None of that needs a micronova, and all of it is mainstream.

That is a smaller claim than the frontier makes and a much larger one than the dismissals allow. It is also, on the evidence, the true one.
