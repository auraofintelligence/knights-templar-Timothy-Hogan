# 05. The dismissal catalogue

*Fourteen stock moves, and what is wrong with each. This is the file the repo exists for.*

Every one of these has been said in place of an argument. Some of them contain a real objection somewhere inside, and where they do, this file points at the good version instead of the lazy one. The good versions live in [`06-objections-that-land.md`](06-objections-that-land.md).

A note on what this file is not. It is not a claim that the micronova is right. Several of these dismissals reach a defensible conclusion by an indefensible route, and a bad argument for a true conclusion is still a bad argument. The point is that the conversation never gets past them, so the actual question never gets asked.

---

### 1. "The Sun cannot go nova, it has no binary companion."

**Answers a claim nobody made.** A classical nova requires a white dwarf with a degenerate surface accreting hydrogen from a companion. The proposed event is not that. Correct physics, wrong target.

**The good version:** the proposed solar mechanism has no worked model, and borrowing the white dwarf's name does not import the white dwarf's physics. Say that instead.

### 2. "Micronova is a made-up word."

**False.** *Nature*, April 2022, Scaringi et al. It has been in the astronomical literature for four years and now appears in classification papers as standard vocabulary.

**The good version:** the 2022 micronova is a different object, so the word's adoption is not evidence for the solar claim.

### 3. "Ben Davidson is a lawyer, not a scientist."

**An ad hominem, and a tell.** Credentials are not evidence and never have been. When this appears in an answer it is usually doing the work that a citation should be doing.

It is also selectively applied. Nobody discounts a correct claim from an amateur astronomer, and the history of the field is full of them. If a specific claim is wrong, name it and show why.

### 4. "It's pseudoscience."

**A verdict standing in for an argument.** The word describes a method, not a conclusion, so it has to be earned by showing the method failing: unfalsifiable claims, moved goalposts, ignored disconfirmation.

That case can partly be made here, and it should be made properly rather than asserted. The forecast window has moved more than once, which is a real methodological problem. Say *that*.

### 5. "There is no evidence."

**False, and lazy in a specific way.** Miyake events, superflare statistics, Laschamps, measured field decay, the South Atlantic Anomaly. All mainstream, all published, all relevant.

What is actually meant is usually "there is no evidence for the *mechanism*", which is a defensible sentence and a different one. Say the sentence you mean.

### 6. "There's no 12,000-year cycle in the record."

**Nearly right, and usually said in a way that is not.** The cadence is not established. But most of the rungs in the ladder are real published proposals sitting on a known list of unconfirmed excursions, so "there is nothing there" is wrong ([`03-the-excursion-record.md`](03-the-excursion-record.md)). And "no cycle has been demonstrated" is a different claim from "the record shows there is no cycle"; only the first is supportable, because palaeo records are sparse and dating uncertainty grows with depth.

### 7. "That's just the Younger Dryas impact hypothesis, which was debunked."

**Two errors.** They are separate claims, and the micronova argument does not depend on an impact. And "debunked" overstates a live dispute, though the direction of travel is real: Hiawatha crater dated to ~58 Ma, the platinum spike reattributed to Icelandic fissure volcanism, and two supporting papers retracted in 2025 and 2026.

**The good version:** the impact hypothesis has been losing ground, so leaning on it as corroboration now costs more than it pays.

### 8. "Scientists would have noticed."

**Assumes the conclusion, and the record is against it.** Miyake events were sitting in tree rings until 2012. Superflare frequency on Sun-like stars was underestimated by one to two orders of magnitude until 2024. The pole motion forced the first out-of-cycle World Magnetic Model update in 2019.

Noticing is a process with a rate, and on this exact topic the rate has been slow and the corrections have gone one way.

### 9. "The magnetic field has been weakening for ages, it's normal."

**True and incomplete.** The dipole has decayed at roughly 5% per century since 1840 and is about 9% below the 1840 value, and the field has been through many such episodes. Normality is the right first read.

But normal is not the same as harmless. The technological exposure is new: an ordinary storm against a weaker field, striking a grid with long conductors and large transformers, is not a scenario the geological record can reassure us about.

### 10. "A pole reversal takes thousands of years, nothing to worry about."

**Conflates reversal with excursion, and overstates the timing consensus.** Excursions are faster and recover to the same polarity. And the field-change rate literature has been revised towards faster, not slower. The point is not the flip; it is the intensity minimum on the way.

### 11. "Correlation is not causation."

**True, and often deployed as a thought-terminator.** The frontier argument is explicitly a coincidence-counting argument, so this objection has to do actual work: show that the correlations are artefacts of selection or dating, or offer a better account of the co-occurrence.

**The good version:** the events were dated and selected by people who knew what the pattern was supposed to be, so the pattern may be in the selection. That is a real objection and a devastating one. Make it.

### 12. "Peer review would have caught it."

**Both a bad argument and, here, a false one.** Solar forcing of climate oscillations, solar modulation of seismicity, cosmic-ray effects on cloud and volcanic processes: all have peer-reviewed literature, much of it contested, some of it cited in the frontier material accurately. The dispute is over weight and interpretation, not over whether anything is published.

### 13. "This is doomsday prepper stuff."

**Guilt by association, and it smuggles in a false link.** The physics claim and the preparation advice are separable. Grid hardening, transformer spares, local water, local food and offline capability are justified by the Carrington-class risk alone, which is a mainstream planning assumption for governments and insurers.

**The move to notice:** the preparation is dismissed by attacking the physics, and the physics is dismissed by attacking the preparation. Neither is an argument about either.

### 14. "It's a YouTube channel."

**Not an argument.** Where the material publishes is not a property of whether it is right. The channel cites primary literature with DOIs throughout the books, which is checkable, and checking it is the work. Some of the citations support what they are said to support and some do not, and only reading them tells you which.

---

## The pattern underneath

Ten of these fourteen share one structure: **they attack the wrapper and report it as a refutation of the contents.** The catastrophe mythology, the CIA chapter, the prepping advice, the channel, the author's degree. That heuristic is cheap and usually right in the wild, which is exactly why it is dangerous here, where it is being applied to a parcel that contains testable physical claims.

The other four are real objections in bad clothes. Wearing better clothes, they are in the next file, and two of them are close to decisive.
