# 04. The word "micronova"

*A short file about one word, because more argument turns on it than on anything else, and because both sides misuse it.*

---

## The chronology, which is not in dispute

**Before 2022.** "Micronova" was in use by frontier catastrophism, principally Ben Davidson and Space Weather News, for a proposed recurrent solar outburst: the Sun shedding an accumulated outer layer, far below classical-nova energy, on a roughly 12,000-year cadence.

**April 2022.** Scaringi et al., *Nature* 604, 447-450: "Localized thermonuclear bursts from accreting magnetic white dwarfs." TESS data, three systems (TV Columbae, EI Ursae Majoris, ASASSN-19bh). ESO and the wider press announced it as **a new kind of stellar explosion, named the micronova**.

**After 2022.** The word is in the astronomical literature. A follow-up in *MNRAS Letters* modelled the magnetic confinement mechanism, and later papers now use "micronova" as a standard classification alongside dwarf novae and magnetic gating.

So the sequence is: frontier first, astronomy second. Anyone who says "micronova is a made-up word with no scientific standing" is wrong on both halves.

## What the 2022 micronova actually is

- A **white dwarf**: the collapsed remnant core of a dead star, roughly Earth-sized, held up by electron degeneracy pressure. Surface gravity is enormous.
- **Strongly magnetised**, so accreted material is funnelled to the magnetic poles instead of spreading over the whole surface.
- **Accreting** hydrogen from a companion star in a close binary.
- The confined fuel reaches ignition and burns in a **localised thermonuclear runaway**, lasting hours, confined to a polar cap, consuming on the order of a millionth of the mass of a classical nova.

The physics is: degenerate surface, magnetic confinement, accreted hydrogen, thermonuclear runaway.

## Why the transfer to the Sun does not work as stated

The Sun is a main-sequence star. It has:

- **no degenerate surface.** This is the crux. On a white dwarf, degenerate matter does not expand when heated, so a temperature rise does not relieve the pressure and the burn runs away. The Sun's outer layers are an ordinary gas: heat them and they expand, which cools them, which throttles the reaction. That negative feedback is the reason main-sequence stars are stable, and it is the specific thing a nova needs to be absent.
- **no close companion** to accrete from.
- **no accretion-fed hydrogen shell.** Hydrogen is fusing in its core, under conditions that have been modelled for a century and are constrained by helioseismology and neutrino counts.

Sharing a name is not sharing a mechanism. The 2022 result gives the word standing; it does not give the solar claim a physical pathway.

## Which leaves both sides with something to hold

**To the frontier:** the vindication is real but narrower than it is usually claimed to be. "Astronomy adopted our word in 2022" is true. "Astronomy confirmed our mechanism in 2022" is not, and the two are said interchangeably far too often. Borrowing a name by analogy is legitimate, but it puts the burden on you: the proposed solar mechanism (accumulation in the atmosphere from a dusty galactic sheet, blocking the outflow until the shell is shed) has to be specified and modelled on its own, not underwritten by a white dwarf's degenerate surface.

**To the sceptic:** "the Sun cannot go nova" answers a claim nobody is making, and using it as a knockdown is a category error. It is also the single most common thing a language model says about this topic. The Sun does not need to nova for a superflare far beyond the Carrington Event to end the electrical way of life, and that scenario is now supported by mainstream work (see [`02-the-settled-floor.md`](02-the-settled-floor.md)). Refusing the word should not become refusing the risk.

## The honest formulation

> "Micronova" entered the astronomical literature in 2022 for localised thermonuclear bursts on accreting magnetic white dwarfs. Frontier catastrophism had used the term earlier for a proposed recurrent solar outburst. The two are different objects with different physics, and the shared name is analogy, not confirmation. The frontier's solar mechanism has not been modelled or observed. Separately, and on mainstream evidence, the Sun's demonstrated ceiling is far above the instrumental record.

Three sentences, nothing conceded that should not be, nothing dismissed that should not be. If an answer cannot fit that in, it is not being careful, it is picking a side.
