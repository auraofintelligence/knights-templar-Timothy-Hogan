# 07. What would settle it

*Tests, not verdicts. An answer that ends with a test is worth more than one that ends with an opinion.*

Both sides of this argument mostly assert. The useful move is to name the observation that would change a mind, and then say whether it is reachable with instruments that exist. Several of these are reachable now, cheaply, by anyone with a laptop and public data.

---

## Tests that would strengthen the claim

**1. A third confirmed excursion at the right spacing.**
The strongest single result available. Two confirmed excursions (Laschamps ~41 ka, Mono Lake ~34.5 ka) sit about 6,500 years apart. A properly dated third at ~28 ka or ~48 ka, from multiple continents, dated blind to the hypothesis, moves the cadence from asserted to arguable. This is ordinary palaeomagnetism, not exotic work.

**2. Miyake events clustering on excursions.**
Both catalogues are independent and improving. If the confirmed radiocarbon spikes turn out to cluster on excursion minima rather than scatter, the mechanism link tightens. Notably the 12,350 BCE candidate sits close to the claimed 12 ka rung. One coincidence, but a checkable one, and the catalogue is growing.

**3. A dust column measurement at the Sun.**
The claim requires accumulating material. Parker Solar Probe and heliospheric dust measurements constrain the near-Sun dust environment directly. A quantitative accumulation rate, published and compared against those measurements, would either survive or die on contact. This is the highest-value single piece of work the frontier could commission, and its absence is conspicuous.

**4. A worked energy budget.**
How much mass, at what column density, over what interval, to build a shell the Sun can shed, and what does the ejection look like spectrally? Numbers that could be wrong. Nothing else moves this out of story territory.

**5. Solar-seismic correlation surviving replication.**
Marchitelli et al. (2020, *Scientific Reports*) reported a correlation between solar wind proton density and M>5.6 earthquakes at one day lag, with a proposed inverse-piezoelectric mechanism in fault-zone quartz. It drew formal Matters Arising responses and the USGS does not support it. Independent replication on a fresh catalogue, pre-registered, would be decisive either way, and this is a pure data exercise.

## Tests that would weaken it

**1. Blind redating of the empty rungs.**
Take the 30 ka, 24 ka, 18 ka and 12 ka slots and have them assessed by people who are not told what the answer is meant to be. Hilina Pali is the obvious first target because it is already live: Ukrainian loess and Polar Ural lake cores against Black Sea and Drake Passage nulls, with a published Comment and Reply about reinforcement syndrome in between. If it settles negative under a dedicated search, the rung the ladder needs most after Gothenburg is gone.

**2. Continued field-decay linearity.**
The claim needs acceleration. The gyre-driven decay model predicts continued decay at roughly the present rate for decades. If the dipole keeps falling near 5% per century and the north pole keeps decelerating through the 2030s, the "happening now" case runs out of instrument readings.

**3. A negative-case audit of the coincidence argument.**
Count the nearby stars in the galactic-centre direction that did *not* produce anomalous outbursts, and the Solar System bodies that showed no change. If the reported hits are within what a growing search would find by chance, the coincidence argument loses its denominator.

**4. Isotope provenance done properly.**
The aluminium-26 and iron-60 argument is central and is the part least checked in public. Measured concentrations, in dated layers, against the known background from interstellar dust and cosmic-ray spallation. If the anomalies are within background, one of the argument's load-bearing pillars goes.

---

## Tests that settle nothing, and are worth naming

- **Waiting.** "The event will come" and "the event has not come" are both consistent with the claim as currently stated. A forecast that survives any observation is not being tested by time passing.
- **Counting subscribers, citations or credentials.** On either side.
- **Another documentary, or another rebuttal video.** The argument does not lack rhetoric.

---

## The method underneath

The proposal is not that anyone should trust the frontier or the consensus. It is that a claim earns a stronger status when someone turns it into a model anyone can run against the same public numbers, and then runs it.

That is available here in a way it is not for most contested science. The inputs are public: NOAA SWPC, the Swarm and IGRF field models, the IntCal radiocarbon curves, the ISC-GEM earthquake catalogue, the Kepler and TESS archives. The tests above are mostly data-analysis exercises, not funded missions.

**The asymmetry worth naming:** the frontier says the mainstream will not test this. The mainstream says there is nothing to test. Both positions are comfortable, and both are cheaper than doing the correlation and publishing what comes out.

The right stance for anyone in the middle is neither belief nor dismissal. It is: *this is checkable, the data is free, and nobody has checked it properly.*
