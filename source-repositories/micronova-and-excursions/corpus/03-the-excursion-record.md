# 03. The excursion record, rung by rung

*The cadence list against the palaeomagnetic literature. Checked one rung at a time, because that is where the argument is actually won or lost, and because nobody on either side seems to do it.*

---

## The list being tested

From the "Disaster Cycle" presentation card, the eight-rung version:

```
48,000 y.a.  LASCHAMP 1
42,000 y.a.  LASCHAMP 2
36,000 y.a.  MONO LAKE
30,000 y.a.  MICHOACAN
24,000 y.a.  LAKE MUNGO
18,000 y.a.  HILINA PALI
12,000 y.a.  GOTHENBURG
 6,000 y.a.  NOAH EVENT
             HAPPENING AGAIN NOW
```

Even spacing, six thousand years a rung. The evenness is the argument: a clean beat implies a driver. So the beat is what has to be checked.

## The two categories that matter

Palaeomagnetism distinguishes excursions that are **in the accepted inventory** from excursions that have been **proposed and not confirmed**. About 13 are well documented across the Brunhes chron (the last ~780 kyr); the five most studied are Mono Lake, Laschamp, Blake, Iceland Basin and Pringle Falls.

Channell et al. (2020) lists the proposed-but-unaccepted excursions for the last 30 kyr:

| Proposed excursion | Age |
|---|---|
| Sterno-Etrussia | ~2.7 ka |
| Tianchi | ~10 ka |
| **Gothenburg** | **~12 ka** |
| Iberian Margin, core MD01-2444 | ~13 ka |
| **Hilina Pali** | **~19 ka** |
| **Lake Mungo** | **~26 ka** |
| Rockall | ~26 ka |

The stated reason for holding them out: these records "have not gained sufficient acceptance to be generally included in the inventory of magnetic excursions", and some have been associated with remagnetisation, sediment disturbance, facies changes, and the **reinforcement syndrome**, which is the field's term for finding an excursion because you went looking for one.

**Three of the ladder's rungs are on that list.** That is a much more interesting result than "the rung is empty", and it is the correction that matters most here.

## Rung by rung

### 48,000 y.a. "Laschamp 1": **unsupported**

No excursion is proposed at this age by anyone. The nearest neighbours are Laschamp at ~41 ka and the Norwegian-Greenland Sea at ~60-64.5 ka.

There *is* a real dating argument inside the Laschamp type locality, and it should be stated rather than waved away. Plenier et al. (2007) date the Olby flow near 37 ka against Laschamps normal-polarity flows at 41.9 ka, with the Royat flow only marginally intermediate and the event ending no younger than 33.3 ka. So the Chaîne des Puys record is not a single clean point. But that is a dispute about the duration and internal structure of **one** event, not evidence for a second event six thousand years earlier.

### 42,000 y.a. Laschamp: **accepted**

Dated to **42,200-41,500 years ago**. Recorded in volcanic rocks and sediments on every continent, reproduced in global field models rather than resting on any one site, and the first excursion ever recognised. The field weakened by as much as **90 per cent**. Discovered in the Laschamps and Olby flows near Clermont-Ferrand in the 1960s. VGP positions persisted for an estimated 440 years in Antarctic records.

It is the tie point everything between roughly 35 and 50 ka gets dated against, in marine sediment, ice cores and lava alike. Not in doubt.

### 36,000 y.a. Mono Lake: **accepted, with a live dispute about its own type locality**

In the accepted inventory and one of the five most studied. Recovered far from where it was named, including Drake Passage sediments (Nowaczyk et al. 2025). Sits nearer **34.5 ka** than 36 ka.

The dispute is worth knowing because it cuts against tidy dating claims generally. Kent et al. (2002), "Laschamp Excursion at Mono Lake?", argued the palaeomagnetic feature in the Wilson Creek Formation is correlative with Laschamp, giving 38-41 ka. Later work found anomalously old argon ages implying inherited sanidine, and favoured 30-34 ka. The current position: Laschamp and Mono Lake are widely agreed to be **distinct** excursions, and which of them is written in the Wilson Creek beds is still contested.

### 30,000 y.a. "Michoacan": **not in the literature**

No excursion of this name appears anywhere, confirmed or proposed. Palaeomagnetic work in the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt has proposed excursions, including an intra-Jaramillo event, but at around a million years. The studies covering the last 30,000 years in the Michoacán-Guanajuato field are palaeosecular variation records from radiocarbon-dated lava flows, reporting the dipole moment rising gradually across the period with a peak near 9 ka.

**Verdict: the name is carrying more than any record does.** If there is a source for it, it has not surfaced, and the burden sits with whoever put it on the ladder.

### 24,000 y.a. Lake Mungo: **proposed, unconfirmed, and correctly placed**

This is where the first pass of this file was wrong and the correction runs in the ladder's favour.

Barbetti and McElhinny (1976) recorded archaeomagnetic directions from Aboriginal fireplaces and oven stones along the ancient shoreline in south-western New South Wales, with departures up to 120 degrees from the axial dipole direction. They found **two** episodes: a main excursion between 30,780 ± 520 and 28,140 ± 370 radiocarbon years BP, and a **second around 26,000 radiocarbon years BP** with intensity falling to roughly a third of present.

Channell et al. (2020) carry it as **"Lake Mungo ~26 ka"**. So the ladder's 24 ka rung is about where the standard reviews put the event, not six thousand years off it. (Note that the original ages are uncalibrated radiocarbon, so the calendar ages sit older; that cuts against precision on this rung in a different direction.)

What it lacks is confirmation. Follow-up work in *Nature* (1977) modelled it as a local source in the outer core, an eccentric radial dipole or current loops, which is a way of saying the signal may be regional. And it sits on the unconfirmed list. A second candidate, Rockall, sits at the same ~26 ka, which is either corroboration or a warning about how many proposals cluster there.

### 18,000 y.a. Hilina Pali: **proposed, unconfirmed, and actively contested right now**

The second correction, also in the ladder's favour. The first pass called this rung empty. It is not empty, it is a live fight.

- **Nawrocki et al. (2018)**, *Boreas*: reversed directions in loess at Rivne, western Ukraine, dated to c. 18 ka, published as a possible Hilina Pali record, with possible self-reversal discussed.
- A formal published **Comment** on that paper, titled around whether Hilina Pali is "becoming another example of the reinforcement syndrome", plus a published **Reply**.
- **Liu et al. (2018)**, *EPSL* 492, 174-185: a 20-15 ka high-resolution record from Black Sea sediments, titled "no evidence for the 'Hilina Pali excursion'?", suggesting it may instead be a pronounced secular variation feature.
- **Nowaczyk et al. (2025)**, Drake Passage: no directional or palaeointensity evidence, and states in the same breath that the postulated excursions "may have been only of regional relevance and/or were too short to be recorded by Drake Passage sediments".
- **Haflidason et al. (2022)**, *Journal of Quaternary Science*: 24,000-year cores from two Polar Ural lakes, "correlated with palaeomagnetic inclination records with a distinct event about 20 000 years ago".
- **Scheidt et al. (2025)**, *JGR Solid Earth*: high-resolution cores from Bolshoye Shchuch'ye and Maloye Shchuch'ye in the Polar Urals, reconstructing secular variation and relative palaeointensity for 23.6-14.8 ka cal BP, explicitly on the proposed Hilina Pali excursion.

**Verdict: contested, with papers published against each other in the same decade.** "No evidence" is the finding at particular sites, not a universal verdict, and the negative papers say so themselves.

### 12,000 y.a. Gothenburg: **proposed, unconfirmed, longest-standing**

Mörner, *Quaternary Research* (1977), from southern Scandinavia: an excursion lasting no more than about a thousand years, generating striking inclination changes. Claimed since in Barents Sea bottom sediments, and used as a chronological marker for the Allerød interstadial in the Central Russian Upland. Never accepted into the inventory, and on Channell's unconfirmed list at ~12 ka.

It is the rung the argument needs most, because it is the one that pairs with the Younger Dryas, the megafaunal collapse and the last claimed micronova. It is also the one that has been sitting unconfirmed the longest, which is itself a signal.

### 6,000 y.a. "Noah": **an inference, presented as one**

The 2022 Supplement is open that the ~6 ka event is absent from the official Heinrich list, and argues it is invisible because it fell in an interglacial rather than a glacial. The supporting evidence offered is circumstantial: the greenest Green Sahara epoch at 6 ka, volcanic evidence at 6 ka, and a Holocene geomagnetic dip whose datasets scatter between 4.5 ka and 7.5 ka and average near 6 ka.

Averaging datasets that disagree by three thousand years, and then treating the average as a dated event, is the weak step. It is not fabrication, and the book does not hide the scatter. No excursion is claimed here by anyone.

| 36 ka Mono Lake | **accepted** (~34.5 ka), type-locality dating contested |
| 30 ka "Michoacan" | **unsupported**, not in the literature under any status |

| 18 ka Hilina Pali | **proposed, unconfirmed, actively contested** (carried at ~19 ka) |

| 6 ka "Noah" | inference from proxies, not an excursion claim |

**Two accepted, three published-but-unconfirmed, two with nothing behind them, one inference.**

## What this does and does not do to the argument

**It does not falsify the micronova.** Unconfirmed means not demonstrated, not demonstrated absent. Dating uncertainty grows with depth, the record is patchy because it depends on what preserved and what got sampled, and the 2025 Drake Passage null result explicitly allows that the events were regional or too brief to register there.

**It does correct the harshest version of the sceptical case.** Most of these rungs are not invented. They are named, published, proposed excursions from working palaeomagnetists. On five of the eight rungs the dispute is confirmation, not existence, and anyone saying "there is nothing there" has not read Channell's list.

**But the metronome still goes**, and for a better reason than empty rungs. Take every excursion proposed for the last 30 kyr, including the unconfirmed ones the ladder depends on, and they land at roughly 2.7, 10, 12, 13, 19, 26 and 26 ka. That is not a beat. It is a scatter, with two candidates nearly on top of each other and a long gap where the ladder wants a rung. **You cannot accept the unconfirmed events that fill the ladder and reject the unconfirmed events that break it.** One standard of proof, applied to all of them, either way you set the bar.

**And it points at the honest version of the work.** Three of the rungs are live research rather than closed questions, and Hilina Pali has people publishing against each other in the same journals this decade. Going and settling one of them, with the dating done blind to what the answer is supposed to be, is ordinary palaeomagnetism and more interesting than the argument it would settle.
