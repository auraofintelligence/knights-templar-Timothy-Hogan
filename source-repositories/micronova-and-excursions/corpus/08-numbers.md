# 08. Numbers to get right

*The precision ledger. Contested figures with the best current value beside them, so that nobody on either side gets ambushed by a number they did not check.*

Getting a number wrong in a live argument costs more than the number is worth. It hands the other side a free win and lets them retire the whole conversation on one correction. Most of the corrections below run against the frontier's stated figures, and carrying them is what makes the rest of the case defensible.

---

## Earth's magnetic field

| Claim in circulation | Best current value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| "5% of the field lost per decade" | **~5% per century** since 1840 | The per-decade figure appears to come from reading a regional South Atlantic Anomaly rate, or a "weakening 10x faster" headline, as a global dipole rate. |
| "Earth is down 20-25%" | **~9% weaker** than 1840 | Systematic measurement began 1840 (Gauss). |
| "The field lost 10% by 2000, 15% by 2010" | not supported | The decay has been close to steady over the instrumental period. |
| "The north pole is accelerating" | **decelerating** | Peaked near 55-60 km/yr in the 1990s-2000s, now ~35 km/yr, the largest recorded deceleration, confirmed WMM 2025. |
| "The 2019 model update was unprecedented" | **true** | First ever out-of-cycle World Magnetic Model update, driven by unforeseen pole motion. |
| "The South Atlantic Anomaly is growing" | **true** | Expanded by an area near half the size of continental Europe since 2014, and developing a second lobe. |
| "A Pacific counterpart appeared from 2017" | **partly supported** | Regional field acceleration over the Pacific is documented; calling it a second anomaly of the same kind overstates it. |
| "The reversal moment is 2040-2060" | **not supported** | No accepted method forecasts a reversal date. The gyre model projects continued decay at roughly the present rate for decades. Note also that an excursion is not a reversal. |

**What survives:** the field is measurably weakening, faster in places than globally, the anomaly is real and growing, and the pole motion was strange enough to break a navigation model. That is a real story. It does not need the inflated version, and the inflated version is what gets the real one dismissed.

## The Sun

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Solar events far larger than Carrington have occurred | **confirmed.** Miyake events, five well confirmed, ~10 identified across 15,000 years. |
| The largest known is ~14,300 years ago (12,350 BCE) | **candidate, strong.** Subfossil Scots pine, French Alps. Awaiting independent confirmation. |
| Sun-like stars superflare roughly once per century | **confirmed.** Vasilyev et al., *Science*, Dec 2024. 2,889 superflares on 2,527 of 56,450 Sun-like stars. |
| Previous superflare estimates were 10-100x too low | **confirmed**, same paper. |
| "The 1859 Carrington Event was a superflare" | **overstated.** Carrington is the largest event of the instrumental era and well below the Miyake events and the Kepler superflare threshold. |
| "The 1859 flare caused a 3-5 degree temperature spike" | **not established.** Cited to a 2017 *ApJ* paper in the Supplement; the attribution is contested and should not be quoted as settled. |
| "The Sun releases an X100 flare every 1,500 years and X1000 every 6,000" | **not established.** This is the cadence claim in solar clothing, and inherits the cadence's problems. |
| Micronova is a published astronomical term | **confirmed**, 2022, for a different object. |

## Adjacent claims worth pricing correctly

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Laschamps coincided with megafaunal extinction and Neanderthal decline | **disputed.** Cooper et al., *Science* 2021 ("Adams Event"). Drew formal Technical Comments in *Science*; critics showed the extinctions and Neanderthal decline were already under way millennia earlier. The authors' response reframed Laschamps as a common endpoint rather than the driver. |
| Younger Dryas impact hypothesis | **losing ground.** Hiawatha crater dated ~58 Ma by two methods. Platinum spike reinterpreted as Icelandic fissure volcanism, occurring ~45 years after Younger Dryas onset and lasting ~14 years, which argues against an instantaneous event. Two supporting papers retracted (Tell el-Hammam 2025, Baffin Bay 2026). |
| Solar activity correlates with large earthquakes | **contested, published.** Marchitelli et al. 2020 *Scientific Reports*; formal Matters Arising responses; USGS does not support it. Live, not settled, either way. |
| 1,500-year Bond / Dansgaard-Oeschger cycle | **increasingly questioned.** Obrochta et al. 2012 re-examined Site 609 and found the ~1,500-year oscillation largely limited to a short, poorly dated interval, and possibly an admixture of ~1,000 and ~2,000-year cycles. The 6,000-year Heinrich harmonic inherits this. |
| Solar forcing of ENSO, NAO, Walker circulation | **real research area, contested weight.** Published literature exists and is cited accurately in the Supplement. The dispute is about magnitude relative to other forcings, not about existence. |
| Scholz's star passed the Solar System ~70 ka | **confirmed** as a close stellar passage. Identifying it with "Nibiru" is not a scientific claim. |

## Rules of thumb

1. **Never quote a field-decay number without saying global or local.** Most of the confusion in this argument lives in that one distinction.
2. **Never say "reversal" when you mean "excursion".** They differ in duration, in outcome, and in what the record supports.
3. **Never cite the Younger Dryas impact hypothesis as support.** It costs more than it pays now.
4. **Never present a proxy inference as a dated event.** The ~6 ka "Noah" rung is the case in point.
5. **Cite the year with the claim.** This field moves. The 2024 superflare paper changed the picture; the 2025 WMM changed another part of it.
