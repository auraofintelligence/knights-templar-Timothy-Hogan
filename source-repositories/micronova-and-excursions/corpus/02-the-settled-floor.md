# 02. The settled floor

*What mainstream, peer-reviewed science already grants. None of this is fringe. None of it requires the micronova to be true. All of it has to be on the table before anyone says "no evidence".*

The floor matters because most arguments about this never reach it. The dismissal arrives so early that the conceded ground is never surveyed, and the conversation proceeds as though the frontier is claiming things science denies. On these five items, science does not deny them. It publishes them.

---

## 1. The Sun produces particle events far beyond anything ever measured

**Miyake events.** In 2012 Fusa Miyake found a sharp one-year rise in radiocarbon in Japanese cedar rings at 774-775 CE. It has since been confirmed worldwide in tree rings, corals and ice cores, and matched to beryllium-10 and chlorine-36. The cause is a solar particle event roughly an order of magnitude beyond the strongest of the instrumental era.

Well-confirmed events, each above a 1% rise in radiocarbon over two years:

| Event | Note |
|---|---|
| 7176 BCE | confirmed |
| 5259 BCE | confirmed 2022 |
| 664-663 BCE | often written "660 BCE" |
| 774-775 CE | fastest confirmed rise |
| 993-994 CE | confirmed |

Further candidates awaiting independent confirmation include 12,350 BCE, 5410 BCE, 1052 CE and 1279 CE. About ten such events have been identified across the last 15,000 years.

**The 12,350 BCE candidate** was reported from subfossil Scots pine in the French Alps, dating to about 14,300 years before present, in the Bølling-Allerød. On present analysis it is the largest known event of its kind.

**Why this is load-bearing:** the Carrington Event of 1859 is the usual benchmark for "worst case". Miyake events are considerably larger, and they are not rare enough to be dismissed. The instrumental record is roughly 170 years long. It is a bad sample of a process with millennial structure.

## 2. Sun-like stars superflare, and more often than we thought

Vasilyev et al., *Science*, 13 December 2024: **"Sun-like stars produce superflares roughly once per century."**

Kepler photometry, 56,450 stars with Sun-like temperature and variability, 2,889 superflares found on 2,527 of them. Events above 10³⁴ erg occur roughly once per century per star, which is **ten to a hundred times more frequent than previous estimates**.

The paper's own framing: if the Sun behaves like the stars in the sample, it could produce superflares at a similar rate.

**Why this is load-bearing:** it removes "the Sun does not do that" from the table. It does not establish a micronova. It establishes that the ceiling on solar behaviour is higher and closer than the textbook picture carried for decades, and that the correction came from the mainstream, not from the frontier.

## 3. Geomagnetic excursions are real, global and documented

An excursion is a rapid, large departure of the field from its normal geometry, with a deep intensity minimum, which then recovers to the **same** polarity. It is not a reversal, and conflating the two is a common error in both directions.

Three excursions in the last 70,000 years are well established:

| Excursion | Age | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Norwegian-Greenland Sea | ~64.5 ka | established |
| **Laschamp** | **42.2-41.5 ka** | best documented on Earth |
| Mono Lake | ~34.5 ka | established |

About **13** excursions are well documented across the Brunhes chron (the last ~780 kyr). The five most studied are Mono Lake, Laschamp, Blake, Iceland Basin and Pringle Falls.

During Laschamp the field weakened by as much as **90 per cent**. It is recorded in volcanic rocks and sediments from every continent, reproduced in global field models (LSMOD.2, GGF100k) rather than resting on single-site records, and it is the tie point everything between roughly 35 and 50 ka gets dated against.

**The distinction that matters downstream:** an excursion being *proposed* is not the same as it being *accepted*. Channell et al. (2020) list seven proposed for the last 30 kyr that have not made it into the inventory, and three of them are rungs on the cadence ladder. See [`03-the-excursion-record.md`](03-the-excursion-record.md).

**Why this is load-bearing:** the planet's magnetic shield is not a constant. It has failed, repeatedly, within the human era, and the record of it is mainstream palaeomagnetism.

## 4. The field is weakening now, and the poles are moving

| Measure | Best current value |
|---|---|
| Axial dipole strength vs 1840 | about **9% weaker** |
| Decay rate since 1840 | about **5% per century** |
| South Atlantic Anomaly | expanded by an area near half the size of continental Europe since 2014; a second lobe developing |
| Fastest-weakening region | Atlantic south-west of Africa, accelerating since 2020 |
| North magnetic pole peak speed | about 55 to 60 km/yr in the 1990s-2000s |
| North magnetic pole now | about **35 km/yr**, the largest deceleration on record |
| Model updates | first ever out-of-cycle World Magnetic Model update, 2019 |

The 2016 *Nature Communications* work attributes the decay to a planetary-scale gyre in the liquid outer core, with the South Atlantic Anomaly's asymmetry essential to the mechanism, and concludes the current episode of decay looks set to continue for at least the next few decades.

**Why this is load-bearing, in both directions:** the changes are real and were significant enough to force an unscheduled navigation-model update, which is not a thing that happens for nothing. And the numbers are smaller than the frontier states, and the pole is slowing rather than accelerating. Both facts belong in any honest answer. See [`08-numbers.md`](08-numbers.md).

## 5. "Micronova" is a published astronomical term

Scaringi et al., *Nature* 604, 447-450, 20 April 2022: **"Localized thermonuclear bursts from accreting magnetic white dwarfs."** TESS photometry, three systems (TV Columbae, EI Ursae Majoris, ASASSN-19bh). Hours-long thermonuclear bursts confined to the magnetic poles of a white dwarf whose strong field funnels accreted material there. A follow-up in *MNRAS Letters* modelled the magnetic confinement.

The frontier used "micronova" for the proposed solar event years before this. Astronomy then adopted the word for a different object.

**Why this is load-bearing:** the history is real, and it is regularly denied. It is also regularly over-claimed. The 2022 object is a degenerate star's surface burning accreted hydrogen; the Sun is a main-sequence star with no degenerate surface. Shared vocabulary is not shared physics. See [`04-the-word-micronova.md`](04-the-word-micronova.md).

---

## What the floor does and does not buy

**It buys:** the Sun has a much larger tail than the instrumental record shows; the magnetic shield fails periodically; the shield is weakening now; and the vocabulary dispute is settled in the frontier's favour as a matter of chronology.

**It does not buy:** a 12,000-year cycle, a galactic trigger, crustal displacement, or a solar shell ejection. Those live on higher layers and have to be argued there.

Anyone who says the floor is empty has not looked. Anyone who says the floor is the whole building is not being careful either.
