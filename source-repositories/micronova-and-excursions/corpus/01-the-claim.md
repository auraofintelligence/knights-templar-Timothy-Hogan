# 01. The claim, at full strength

*The frontier case, steelmanned. No hedging inserted, no wrapper stripped for comfort. Sources: Ben Davidson, "The Next End of the World" (Space Weather News, 2020); "Observer Supplement 2022/2023" (April 2022); the daily Suspicious0bservers programme at SpaceWeatherNews.com.*

A steelman is not an endorsement. It is the version of the argument you have to beat. Most published rebuttals beat a weaker one.

---

## The one-paragraph version

Earth's long quiet stretches are punctuated by a recurring catastrophe on a roughly 12,000-year cadence, with a ~6,000-year half-beat. The driver is not on Earth. The Solar System periodically crosses the galactic current sheet, a wavy magnetic boundary threading the Milky Way's disc that carries dust and charged material. The crossing does two things to the Sun: it loads its atmosphere with material, and it disrupts its magnetic output. Pressure builds under an increasingly opaque outer layer until the Sun sheds it, in an event the frontier calls a **micronova**: a flash, then hours of particle bombardment, then a shockwave of plasma, dust and debris crossing the inner Solar System. That one event is offered as the single cause of the whole evidence set: the geomagnetic excursion, the impactors, the anomalous isotopes, the flash-frozen megafauna, the great waves, and the abrupt cold. The claim is that the run-up began in 1859 and the event is due this century.

---

## The evidence set the claim is built to explain

The argument's real ambition is not "the Sun might flare". It is Frank Hibben's old challenge: **explain all the evidence with one mechanism.** Six things need explaining together:

1. **Geomagnetic excursions.** Rapid collapses and recoveries of the field, roughly every 10,000 to 15,000 years, each with an intensity minimum that lets space energy into the Earth system.
2. **Impactors.** Craters, microtektites and airburst signatures clustering near some of those excursions. Nothing about a magnetic excursion causes impacts, and nothing about an impact flips a field over decades. Yet they keep company.
3. **Anomalous isotopes.** Aluminium-26, iron-60 and short-lived heavy isotopes found in surge deposits, muck pits and microtektites, with half-lives short enough that they cannot be primordial and cannot have travelled far. A distant supernova is too slow. A near one would have sterilised us.
4. **Instant freeze.** Mammoths recovered with undigested food, frozen fast enough that decay never started. Dust-loading and albedo cooling are too slow to do this.
5. **The great waves.** Surge deposits, marine shells at altitude, granite blocks on mountains, deep-sea canyons cut off continental shelves. Water leaving its bed requires the crust to shift, the tilt to change, or land to rise and fall.
6. **The stories.** A dark or black Sun, the Sun standing still or rising in the wrong place, a long night, an inundation, and a rebirth, told independently across cultures that had no contact.

Any one of these has a local explanation. The frontier's point is that a set of local explanations that happen to co-occur, repeatedly, is not an explanation.

## The proposed chain, link by link

**Link 1: the galactic current sheet exists.** The heliospheric current sheet is textbook: the Sun's rotating magnetic field forms a wavy boundary that Earth crosses every week or so, and each crossing can deliver a geomagnetic disturbance. Parker instability scales. At galactic scale, alternating magnetic field direction across the disc has been detected repeatedly, and the sheet is placed 70 to 160 parsecs above and below the galactic equator.

**Link 2: the crossing loads the Sun.** Unlike the heliospheric sheet, which the solar wind keeps swept clean, the galactic sheet is dusty. Crossing it supplies both accretion material and an electromagnetic disruption of solar output. Either mechanism alone can build accumulation in a stellar atmosphere.

**Link 3: stars erupt when their environment changes, not only when they have a companion.** This is the load-bearing astrophysical claim and 2020 was its best year. A Type Ia supernova was found with no companion. A transient-accretion recurrent nova was found still keeping a regular cycle. Simulations showed the donor in symbiotic multiple-nova systems can be of any type, loosening the standard model. And Sofue reported a star that wandered into a molecular cloud and erupted, leaving a spherical cavity. Environment can trigger an outburst.

**Link 4: the outburst unlocks the crust.** Length-of-day glitches are real and measured, and correlate with two things: geomagnetic jerks from the core, and strong solar storms. Both reach the low-velocity zone at the crust-mantle boundary, where rock sits on plastic rock. A large enough event delivers both at once. Yelverton's bench work shows olivine translocating on electrical discharge and water in rock moving against gravity in under a quarter of a second. Scale the mechanism, unlock the crust, and the oceans leave their beds.

**Link 5: the shell brings the rest.** Debris flung from the shed layer is the impactors. Nova-process nucleosynthesis is the isotopes. Atmospheric depressurisation under a weakened field, plus supercooled upper air and an ice-water inundation, is the instant freeze. Dust loading plus albedo is the ice age.

**Link 6: the run-up is observable now, and it is.** The field has weakened since 1859. The north magnetic pole left its meander and ran. The World Magnetic Model required its first unscheduled update. The South Atlantic Anomaly grew and split, and a Pacific counterpart appeared from 2017. Other planets show change. Nearby stars in the direction of the galactic centre, Barnard's Star and Proxima and AD Leonis, produced unprecedented outbursts in sequence.

## The cadence

The 2022 Supplement builds the cadence from Heinrich events, the amplified fourth harmonic of the 1,500-year Dansgaard-Oeschger cycle, and notes that the official Heinrich list runs H0 (~12 ka) through H4 (~36 ka) but is missing the one that should sit at ~6,000 years. It argues the missing event is invisible because it fell in an interglacial rather than a glacial, and points to the greenest Green Sahara epoch at 6 ka, volcanic evidence at 6 ka, and a Holocene geomagnetic dip averaging near 6 ka. Pairing each with a geomagnetic event gives the ladder:

| Years ago | Event named |
|---|---|
| ~6,000 | "Noah" / "Sumerian" |
| ~12,000 | Younger Dryas / Gothenburg |
| ~18,000 | Hilina Pali |
| ~24,000 | Lake Mungo |
| ~30,000 | (unnamed in 2022; "Michoacan" in later material) |
| ~36,000 | Mono Lake |
| ~42,000 | Laschamp 2 |
| ~48,000 | Laschamp 1 |
| now | claimed to be under way |

The eight-rung version with Michoacan named and Laschamps split in two is the later presentation, and is the one on the "Disaster Cycle" documentary card. It is a refinement of the 2022 list, not a different claim.

The mechanism offered for the beat: the Sun's superflare cycle has harmonics. Roughly X100 every 1,500 years, driving a Dansgaard-Oeschger warming. Roughly X1000 every 6,000 years, driving a Heinrich event. The 12,000-year harmonic is the micronova.

## What the claim says about itself

Worth keeping, because both sides tend to drop it:

- The books state plainly that the exact cycle length is disputed even among catastrophists, listing ~11,500 years (Walker), 12,068 years (Vogt), and 21,000 to 26,000 years (precession models) as rival figures.
- They assign a severity score to past excursions and argue the next is likelier to be a 4 or 5 out of 10 than an 8, because the record does not show two extreme events back to back.
- They state that no definitive claim can be made about other planets' magnetism, and that the nearby-star evidence is "even less open to conclusive statements" than the planetary evidence.
- They concede the solar-wind stunting mechanism cannot currently be modelled.

An argument that flags its own weak links is being made in better faith than the summaries of it usually allow.

## The strongest single move in the argument

Not any individual piece of evidence. It is the **coincidence-counting** move: given that excursions, impactors, short-lived isotopes, rapid freezing, inundation deposits and a global mythological record keep arriving together, the null hypothesis has to explain the co-occurrence, not each item separately. A theory that explains all six with one cause is preferable to six unrelated coincidences, unless the coincidences are shown to be an artefact of how the events were dated and selected.

That last clause is where the real argument lives. It is taken up in [`06-objections-that-land.md`](06-objections-that-land.md).
