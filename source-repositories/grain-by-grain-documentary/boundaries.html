Public development posture

Wonder grows stronger beside clear boundaries.

This treatment can be brave, strange and cinematic while staying honest about cultural authority, evidence, consent, privacy, safety and what has not been decided.

GenAI concept image for documentary development.

Eight living boundaries

Keep the audience oriented without closing the frontier.

These boundaries can shape research, design, filming, editing, public copy and companion experiences.

Country and culture

Country is not a backdrop.

Quandamooka cultural material, places, stories and authority sit with appropriate people and processes. Early invitation includes room to shape, limit or refuse use.

Evidence

A repo starts a trail.

A project page can suggest a question, scene or source. Consequential claims return to suitable evidence, dates, places and challenge.

Privacy

Capability does not require exposure.

Private contacts, addresses, medical details, protected wildlife locations, security weaknesses and unapproved footage stay outside the public plan.

Safety

The film is not an emergency manual.

Storm, fire, food, energy, shelter and health scenes can point viewers toward current, suitable public guidance rather than improvising instructions.

Politics, war and AI

Complex systems resist a single villain.

The film can examine power, concentration, conflict, public choices and uncertainty without turning a prototype, party idea or technology company into unquestioned authority.

Frontier theories

Test without reflex dismissal.

A disputed or minority theory can receive a fair account, its strongest available sources and a useful test. Its evidence status remains visible; mainstream consensus is context rather than a veto.

Life worth protecting

Resilience is not permission to control.

Closed loops and deep shelter do not require closed lives. Agency, privacy, culture, beauty, play, dissent, movement and the right to decline remain part of the design load.

Fiction and mystery

Story keeps its name.

Mythology, contact narratives, simulations and science fiction can carry meaning and rehearsal value. They remain visibly distinct from established science and source-backed local claims.

What this public site is not

A map, not a claim of authority.

The project can grow through conversation and source work without presenting an early treatment as more settled than it is.

Not official material

It does not speak for Quandamooka people, QYAC, emergency services, councils, Queensland, Australia, researchers, partner groups or source-repo communities.

Not an approved project

The Dunwich loop, tunnels, industry and generation-city horizon do not show approval, funding, geotechnical proof, cultural authority, site control or delivery commitment.

Not a finished film

The runtime, voices, scenes, title, evidence and release path can change as research, relationships and editorial choices develop.

A release-worthy scene record

Enough context to review the choice.

A compact record can help collaborators see what a scene is doing before footage, claims or sensitive material travel further.

The first respectful conversation

Who could shape this before the camera does?

The public plan names roles, not presumed participants. Invitation and relationship can come before requests for stories, access or endorsement.

Cultural and place authority

Discuss the premise, name, Country framing, story limits, places, archive and participation pathway with suitable Quandamooka authority.

Local safety and systems

Invite people with current experience in emergency preparation, infrastructure, food, energy, communications, wildlife and recovery to challenge the act map.

People represented

Give prospective participants a clear picture of topic, intended audience, edit control boundaries, privacy and possible future use.

Science and uncertainty

Ask suitable specialists to test consequential claims and help the evidence labels remain meaningful on screen.
